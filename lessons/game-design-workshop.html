Game Design Workshop

Tracy Fullerton

2004 · 2024 fifth editionGame design and interactive systems · Playcentric design · Playtesting5–8 minute read

Design from an intended player experience, express it through interacting rules and discover the actual game through cheap prototypes, observed play and disciplined iteration.

Evidence lens. Fullerton presents a practitioner framework refined through game-design teaching and production rather than a controlled causal theory. The playcentric loop, formal elements and observational playtesting are durable design methods, but their effectiveness is context-dependent: player samples, genre knowledge, production constraints and the designer's interpretation all shape what a test reveals. The 2024 fifth edition updates examples and techniques while preserving the core process.

Idea 1

Start with the experience, then test the system #

The idea

Fullerton's playcentric process begins with player experience goals: concise descriptions of what players should think, feel or do during play. A goal such as tense cooperation despite incomplete trust is different from a feature list. It gives the designer a criterion for choosing mechanics and a claim that can be tested with players.

The designer cannot directly manufacture an experience. The designer creates rules, procedures, resources and information; players interact with that system and produce an experience. Prototypes close the gap between intention and effect by showing whether the system reliably invites the targeted behaviour.

Applied interpretation. Write an experience goal as an observable tension or behaviour, then identify the smallest rule interaction that could create it. Treat the goal as a test criterion, not promotional copy.

Why it matters

Teams can agree on a feature while imagining different player experiences. An experience goal makes the hidden disagreement discussable and lets a prototype answer a sharper question than whether the game is generally fun.

Apply it today

Choose one interaction and complete this sentence: players should feel or do ___ because they must choose between ___ and ___.

Name the one rule or information constraint most responsible for that tension and the behaviour you would watch for in a five-minute test.

Caveat or failure mode

A stated experience goal can become a story the team uses to reinterpret any result. Define observable signals before the test, include reactions that would weaken the goal and allow players to create valuable experiences the designer did not predict.

02

Idea 2

Tune relationships among formal elements #

The idea

Fullerton breaks a game's formal core into elements including players, objectives, procedures, rules, resources, conflict, boundaries and outcomes. The value of the vocabulary is relational. A resource is meaningful because rules make it scarce, procedures let players spend it and objectives make the timing consequential.

Dynamic play emerges from those interactions rather than from an isolated mechanic. Changing one rule can alter incentives, information, pacing and viable strategies elsewhere in the system. Designers therefore analyse loops, trade-offs and dominant patterns, not only whether each component works alone.

Applied interpretation. Map one important decision across objective, available action, resource cost, information and consequence. If the preferred action is obvious in every state, adjust a relationship rather than adding decorative complexity.

Why it matters

A feature can be individually elegant yet damage the game by removing a trade-off or creating one dominant strategy. A system map makes second-order consequences easier to anticipate and investigate.

Apply it today

Choose one recurring player decision and write its objective, allowed actions, resource costs, known information and outcome.

Change one relationship on paper and predict which behaviour, strategy or pacing effect should increase and which should decrease.

Caveat or failure mode

A formal map is a model, not the play experience. It can miss emotion, social meaning, accessibility, culture and improvisation. Use it to generate predictions, then let diverse players reveal relationships the diagram omitted.

03

Idea 3

Observe play before defending the design #

The idea

Fullerton treats playtesting as a structured design activity, not simply letting friends play and asking whether they liked it. The designer chooses a question, recruits relevant players, watches what they actually do and records moments of confusion, strategy, emotion and breakdown before explaining the intended solution.

Iteration compares the prediction with observed play, changes the prototype and tests again. Early prototypes should be cheap because their purpose is to make assumptions disposable. A useful result may invalidate a favourite mechanic before production effort turns it into a commitment.

Applied interpretation. Before a test, write one behavioural prediction and one observation that would contradict it. During play, separate what happened, what the player later said and what you infer; change one important variable when possible.

Why it matters

Designers know the rules, intended strategy and unfinished context, so they cannot experience the prototype like a new player. Silent observation exposes gaps that an explanation would accidentally repair.

Apply it today

Write one prediction for a prototype in the form: when players encounter ___, most will do ___ because ___.

Define one contradicting observation, then prepare a note with separate columns for behaviour, player words and your interpretation.

Caveat or failure mode

One session can reflect an unrepresentative player, unclear facilitation or novelty. Observation is not self-interpreting, and changing one variable is not always possible in a connected system. Look for repeated patterns across suitable players and keep alternative explanations alive.

Question 1 of 6 · Idea 1 · ConceptWhat makes a player experience goal useful in Fullerton's process?

A feature list names implementation, not the experience those features are meant to create. The goal should guide rather than freeze the design.

Audience knowledge matters, but a market segment does not state what the system should cause players to think, feel or do.

The goal connects design intention to observable play without claiming the designer can control every response.

Players bring different skills and interpretations. The goal is a testable intention, not a promise of identical experience.

An experience goal describes a target response or situation for players. It guides mechanic choices and supplies a question for playtesting, but it neither fixes every feature nor guarantees a uniform reaction.

Question 2 of 6 · Idea 2 · ConceptWhy does Fullerton analyse formal elements as a connected system?

A cost, action or objective matters because of its relationship to the rest of the formal system.

The framework is a vocabulary for analysing different games, not a required inventory or fixed count.

Formal analysis supports predictions, but players can respond socially and emotionally in ways the map does not contain.

Independent tuning can hide a system effect. Mechanics must be examined in the context that gives them incentives and consequences.

Objectives, rules, resources, information and outcomes give one another meaning. Their interaction produces strategies and dynamics, so local changes must be considered in the larger system and then tested in play.

Question 3 of 6 · Idea 3 · ConceptWhat turns a casual play session into a diagnostic playtest?

Explanation can make the test measure the designer's tutorial rather than whether the game communicates and behaves as intended.

An overall score says little about which interaction produced the response or what to change next.

High fidelity can make some tests realistic, but early polish also raises cost and can distract from the rule under investigation.

Prediction and structured observation make the result comparable and useful for a specific iteration.

A diagnostic test begins with a design question, observes behaviour without repairing the experience through explanation and separates evidence from interpretation. Polish and overall opinion can be useful later but often hide the mechanism being tested.

Question 4 of 6 · Idea 1 · ApplicationA cooperative game should create tension because players need one another but cannot share every clue. Which next step best follows playcentric design?

Art can support emotion, but it does not test whether the information structure produces cooperation and mistrust during play.

The prototype tests the smallest system likely to create the goal and looks for the behaviour that would support or weaken it.

Narrative may justify the rule, but explanation is not evidence that players will experience its tension in the game.

Stated preferences provide context, but they cannot reveal how players behave inside this specific interaction.

The information rule is the proposed mechanism for the experience, so a cheap prototype should expose it to player behaviour. Art, story and surveys may contribute later, but they do not yet test whether the interaction creates the intended tension.

Question 5 of 6 · Idea 2 · ApplicationA strategy game's healing item is always the best use of currency. What is the strongest first design analysis?

Visual treatment can communicate importance, but it does not restore a strategic trade-off in the currency system.

More currencies add complexity without explaining why the existing decision is dominated by one option.

Preference may inform theme, but the design problem is that system incentives make one action mechanically superior.

The map examines the connected elements that make healing the dominant strategy and points to a testable relationship change.

The dominant choice is a relationship problem. Mapping cost, timing, alternatives and outcomes can reveal why one action wins in every relevant state and which connection a prototype should change.

Question 6 of 6 · Idea 3 · ApplicationPlayers repeatedly miss a route the designer considers obvious. What is the best response during the current test?

Observation comes first, and a controlled iteration tests a plausible cause without explaining away the failure.

Instruction may be needed in a later test, but during this question it repairs the exact communication problem being examined.

Genre knowledge may be an alternative explanation, yet it should be tested against the intended audience rather than assumed defensively.

Applying every suggestion confounds the result and may solve symptoms with added clutter rather than identify the important cue.

The missed route is evidence about the current system. Recording the divergence preserves the finding; one targeted change and a retest can show whether the proposed cue addresses it without introducing unnecessary fixes.

Today's experiment · 10 minutes

Run a paper playtest of one decision

  1. Write one experience goal and the player behaviour that would support it.
  2. Represent only the essential objective, choice, resource and consequence with paper, cards or a short verbal rule.
  3. Ask one person to make three decisions while you observe without explaining the preferred strategy.
  4. Record one prediction that held, one surprise and one rule relationship to change before the next test.

Observable result: You finish with observed behaviour and one evidence-linked iteration, not a general opinion about an imagined game.
